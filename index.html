    Parts wall

    Each station shows the factory part first, then every modification part for it: what it looks like, how it works, what you gain and what it costs you. Figures follow the vehicle on the lift.

    How the numbers are made. The dials come from a simplified physics model: power and torque percentages per part, power-to-weight for acceleration, a cube-root power law and a gearing limit for top speed. They show direction and rough size of a change. They are not dyno results. The test strip is calibrated to the same figures, so a perfect launch there reaches 100 km/h in the time the panel shows, and the engine sound is synthesised from cylinder count, revs and the exhaust dial. Real gains depend on the exact part, the tune and the condition of the engine.

    Road law. In India, Section 52 of the Motor Vehicles Act restricts altering a vehicle from its registered specification, and loud or cat-less exhausts are routinely fined and can fail the PUC test. Structural, lighting and emissions changes can also affect insurance and warranty. Check your RTO and insurer before fitting anything.
